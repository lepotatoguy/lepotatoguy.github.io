Research highlights

Reliable learning systems, from lightweight models to certified behavior.

My research asks how much we can trust, prove, and explain about a learning system when labels, samples, or compute are limited. I study parameter-efficient adaptation of foundation models, evidence and evaluation for language-model systems, and decision-making with guarantees, building on earlier work in lightweight computer vision, medical imaging, and cloud computing.

Adaptation & guarantees

Certified parameter-efficient fine-tuning

I study when parameter-efficient adaptation can be trusted, and what can be proven about it. My work characterizes when Bayesian low-rank adapters admit useful generalization certificates, and the exact conditions under which a prefix can stand in for a LoRA adapter inside a frozen attention head.

  • Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning
  • LoRA
  • PAC-Bayes Bounds
  • Transformers

Questions I study

  • When does a calibrated posterior over low-rank adapters yield a useful generalization certificate?
  • Which changes to the prior, the predictor, or complexity accounting actually tighten a certificate?
  • Can a fixed prefix replace a low-rank adapter while the attention head stays frozen?

Evidence & evaluation

Reliable language-model systems

I study how language-model systems should decide whether they have enough evidence to answer, and how to certify a model update when trusted labels are scarce and the evaluator itself, such as an LLM judge, may drift over time.

  • Memory-Augmented LLMs
  • Evidence Sufficiency
  • LLM-as-a-Judge
  • Label-Efficient Evaluation

Questions I study

  • How can an agent tell that the evidence a question needs is no longer in its memory?
  • How should benchmarks build insufficient-evidence examples without leaking the label?
  • When can evaluator errors from earlier audits replace fresh labels in a release decision?

Learning & decisions

Decision-making with guarantees

I work on sequential decision-making that is reliable and transparent: contextual bandits that exploit graph structure, with a simple diagnostic that shows in advance when the method will help; bandits that meet exact exposure floors that can be checked in every period; and approximate behavioral-similarity metrics for Markov decision processes that report checkable bounds on their own error.

  • Contextual Bandits
  • Reinforcement Learning
  • Fairness Constraints
  • Bisimulation Metrics

Questions I study

  • How can graph structure among arms reduce the cost of exploration?
  • How can exposure guarantees be met exactly in every period without giving up regret?
  • When can a faster, approximate computation still certify its own error?

Healthcare AI

Medical image analysis

I worked on medical image analysis through small architectural changes, such as a single involution layer before a CNN that improves classification and segmentation with a negligible number of extra parameters, along with polyp segmentation and eye-tracking-based autism classification.

  • Medical Imaging

Questions I studied

A connected agenda

Keep it lean. Prove it reliable. Make it transparent.

Each thread feeds the next: lean models and cloud systems made learning efficient where compute and bandwidth were scarce, reliability work asks what can be certified when labels or samples run short, and transparency comes from explainable models and guarantees that can be checked, or that hold back an answer when the evidence is not there.
